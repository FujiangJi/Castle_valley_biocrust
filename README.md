## Summary
* Biological soil crusts (biocrusts) are key components of dryland ecosystems, but climate-driven shifts in their community composition remain difficult to monitor beyond field plots. Most remote-sensing studies emphasize categorical classification rather than continuous estimation of the fractional cover of biocrust functional types (BFTs) within mixed surfaces.
* We evaluated a framework for estimating biocrust fractional cover from naturally mixed field spectra (ASD FieldSpec 3, 350–2500 nm) in a long-term (since 2005) climate manipulation experiment at Castle Valley, Utah (Colorado Plateau). The previously documented warming-driven successional regression at this site (Phillips et al., 2022) served as an ecological benchmark.
* Two approaches were compared under identical nested cross-validation: Random Forest (RF) regression on measured spectra, and transfer learning with a one-dimensional convolutional neural network (1D-CNN) pre-trained on synthetic mixtures generated from a local endmember library and fine-tuned on 240 measured spectra.
* Under the successional-stage (4-class) scheme, the fine-tuned 1D-CNN reached _R²_ = 0.584 (early successional, LtCy), 0.576 (late successional, DkCy + lichen + moss), 0.515 (senescent vegetation) and 0.139 (bare substrate), compared with best RF-based _R²_ of 0.542, 0.461, 0.477 and 0.141. Under the individual-BFT (6-class) scheme, estimates were strongest for lightly pigmented cyanobacteria (_R²_ = 0.570), followed by moss (0.443) and darkly pigmented cyanobacteria (0.384), whereas lichen remained difficult to estimate (0.124).
* Model-estimated cover recovered the direction and rank order of the documented treatment effects. Under combined warming and altered precipitation, measured late-successional cover declined by 37.3 ± 19.2 percentage points and early-successional cover increased by 36.3 ± 10.9 percentage points; the corresponding estimates were −22.6 ± 10.4 and +32.5 ± 6.7 percentage points (mean ± SD across five blocks).

## Description of data
The `0_data/` folder contains the raw inputs used in this study:
* `ASD_Spectra/ASD_All_Spectra_PlotLevel.csv` — subplot-level reflectance of naturally mixed surfaces (350–2500 nm, interpolated to 1 nm), 300 spectra (4 treatments × 5 blocks × 15 subplots). Subplots 1–12 of each plot (240 spectra) have paired cover observations and are used for modeling.
* `ASD_Spectra/ASD_All_Spectra_ContactProbe.csv` — pure endmember spectral library: 168 sample-level spectra (each the mean of five contact-probe measurements) in eight categories: darkly pigmented cyanobacteria (DkCy, n = 36), lightly pigmented cyanobacteria (LtCy, n = 36), lichen (n = 36), moss (n = 36), vegetation (n = 9), litter (n = 9), rock (n = 3) and bare soil (n = 3).
* `USGS_Cover_Data/FractionaCover_BySpectra_2021_v2.csv` — point-intercept cover matched to each subplot spectrum (USGS).

Prediction targets:
* **4-class (successional stage) scheme**: late successional (DkCy + lichen + moss), early successional (LtCy), senescent vegetation, bare substrate.
* **6-class (individual BFT) scheme**: DkCy, lichen, moss, LtCy, senescent vegetation, bare substrate.
* *Senescent vegetation* = litter + live + dead vascular vegetation. Because the point-intercept survey records multiple vertical layers, this target can exceed 100%. *Bare substrate* = rock + bare soil.

Processed tables and synthetic mixtures are not included because they are regenerated by the scripts.

## Modeling framework
* **Pure-endmember separability.** RF classification (500 trees, stratified 5-fold cross-validation) of the endmember library under the 4-class and 6-class groupings, with per-band feature importance.
* **Approach 1 — Random Forest regression.** Eight configurations (RF or PCA + RF × reflectance only or reflectance + 9 spectral indices × 4-class or 6-class) evaluated with nested cross-validation: outer `KFold(5, shuffle=True, random_state=123)` for evaluation and inner `KFold(5, random_state=42)` with `RandomizedSearchCV` (60 draws) for tuning. Separate per-target RF models provide per-band feature importance. The 1320–1440 nm and 1770–1960 nm water-absorption regions are excluded (1,839 bands).
* **Approach 2 — Transfer learning (1D-CNN).**
  * *Synthetic mixtures*: 10,000 spectra generated from the eight-category library with linear or bilinear mixing (γ ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 1}); 1 to *k*<sub>max</sub> (4 or 8) categories per pixel, selected uniformly or in proportion to mean field cover; Dirichlet abundances (α ~ U(0.3, 1.0)); within-category variability represented by convex combinations of library spectra and a brightness factor ~ N(1, 0.05).
  * *Network*: three convolutional blocks (kernels 7, 5, 3), adaptive pooling, a 128-unit fully connected layer with dropout, and a linear output layer.
  * *Training and evaluation*: data generation, preprocessing, pre-training and fine-tuning settings are optimized jointly with 60 Optuna trials per scheme, scored only by inner-validation _R²_. Fine-tuning uses the same five outer folds as the RF branch; every inner-fold model starts from the pre-trained weights and is early-stopped on its own inner-validation fold, and each outer fold is predicted by the mean of its five inner-fold models. Outer-test samples are never used for model selection. Results from bilinear mixing are reported in the paper.

## Usage
* Clone this repository
  ```
  git clone https://github.com/FujiangJi/Castle_valley_biocrust.git
  cd Castle_valley_biocrust
  ```
* Build the measured dataset (merges spectra with cover, builds the targets and computes the 9 spectral indices)
  ```
  cd 1_data_processing && python process_measured_data.py
  ```
* Random Forest branch (nested CV of the 8 configurations, per-target feature importance, pure-endmember classification)
  ```
  cd ../2_rf_analysis
  python 2_RF_analysis.py
  python 3_RF_seperate_feature_importance.py
  python 4_RF_pure_endmember_classification.py
  ```
* Transfer-learning branch (example: bilinear mixing, 4-class scheme; repeat for `bilinear_6scheme`, `linear_4scheme`, `linear_6scheme`)
  ```
  cd ../3_synthetic_approach
  python 2_synthetic_datasets.py --priors uniform matched                          # optional: export reference synthetic datasets
  python 3_pretrain_model.py --scheme bilinear_4scheme [--config <config.json>]
  python 4_fine_tune_model.py --scheme bilinear_4scheme [--config <config.json>] --final ensemble
  ```
  Without `--config`, the built-in default configuration is used.
* Generate figures
  ```
  cd ../4_figure_plotting && python Figure3.py    # etc.
  ```
RF outputs are written to `2_results/` and CNN outputs to `3_results/` (created at runtime). All random seeds are fixed (outer folds 123, inner folds 42, training and synthetic data 42). Approximate run times: RF nested CV ~10–17 h (CPU), per-target RF ~1 day (CPU).

## Description of files in this repository
* **`0_data/`**: raw ASD spectra and point-intercept cover data.
* **`1_data_processing/`**
  * **[process_measured_data.py](1_data_processing/process_measured_data.py)**: merges subplot spectra with cover data; builds `frac_Vegetation` (litter + live + dead) and `frac_Bare` (rock + bare soil); computes nine spectral indices (brightness index, NDVI, PRI, NDNI, NDWI, MCARI, CI, SCAI, BSCI); writes identical processed tables for both modeling branches with an md5 manifest.
* **`2_rf_analysis/`**
  * **[1_process_measured_data.py](2_rf_analysis/1_process_measured_data.py)**: same as `1_data_processing/process_measured_data.py`.
  * **[2_RF_analysis.py](2_rf_analysis/2_RF_analysis.py)**: nested-CV multi-output RF and PCA + RF for both schemes and both predictor sets.
  * **[3_RF_seperate_feature_importance.py](2_rf_analysis/3_RF_seperate_feature_importance.py)**: per-target RF models (nested CV) and per-band / per-index feature importance.
  * **[4_RF_pure_endmember_classification.py](2_rf_analysis/4_RF_pure_endmember_classification.py)**: RF classification of the pure endmember library (4-class and 6-class), confusion matrices and feature importance.
* **`3_synthetic_approach/`**
  * **[1_process_measured_data.py](3_synthetic_approach/1_process_measured_data.py)**: same as `1_data_processing/process_measured_data.py`.
  * **[2_synthetic_datasets.py](3_synthetic_approach/2_synthetic_datasets.py)**: exports reference synthetic datasets and a realism summary against the measured spectra (pre-training generates mixtures on the fly).
  * **[3_pretrain_model.py](3_synthetic_approach/3_pretrain_model.py)**: pre-trains the 1D-CNN on synthetic mixtures for one scheme.
  * **[4_fine_tune_model.py](3_synthetic_approach/4_fine_tune_model.py)**: nested-CV fine-tuning on measured spectra; writes `final_model_estimation_<scheme>.csv` (outer-test predictions) and `measured_data_apply_to_pretrained_model_<scheme>.csv` (pre-trained model without fine-tuning).
* **`4_figure_plotting/`**
  * **[Figure1.py](4_figure_plotting/Figure1.py)**: study area and sampling design (Fig. 1).
  * **[Figure2.py](4_figure_plotting/Figure2.py)**: spectral library, measured mixed spectra and cover by treatment (Fig. 2).
  * **[Figure3.py](4_figure_plotting/Figure3.py)**: model comparison, 4-class scheme (Fig. 3).

  * **[Figure5.py](4_figure_plotting/Figure5.py)**: per-band feature importance, reflectance-only RF (Fig. 5).
  * **[Figure5_RF_indices.py](4_figure_plotting/Figure5_RF_indices.py)**: contribution of spectral bands and indices (Fig. S1).

  * **[Figure6.py](4_figure_plotting/Figure6.py)**: measured vs. estimated cover and residuals by block, 4-class scheme (Fig. 6 and Figs. S3–S6).
